**Before paying for a home workout program, write down four things: the amount due now, any future charges, when access ends and who handles billing.** A price expressed “per month” may describe an annual payment divided by twelve. It does not necessarily mean you can pay for just one month.

This guide helps you compare the commitment and find the right cancellation route. To decide whether the exercises, equipment and session length fit your routine, start with our [beginner home workout guide](/metabolism/choosing-beginner-home-workout/).

## Compare the payment commitment, not just the headline price

Keep three arrangements separate when reading an offer:

- **Recurring subscription:** another charge is scheduled unless you stop renewal under its terms. The billing interval could be monthly, quarterly or yearly.
- **Fixed-term access without renewal:** you pay for a stated period and access expires. Check that the checkout actually says it will not renew; “six-month access” alone does not answer that question.
- **Installments for a longer commitment:** payments are spread out, but that does not necessarily make each payment a new, optional month.

That last distinction can matter even when the subscription has a cancellation button. For example, [Google Play's guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481?hl=en) says stopping renewal of a subscription with multiple committed payments does not remove the remaining payments for the current term. Read the terms for the arrangement you are buying.

### Worked example: a lower monthly equivalent can cost more upfront

These are **invented offers in US dollars**, not current prices for FITin56 or any other service. Assume identical features and no taxes, equipment costs, refunds or price changes.

| Hypothetical offer | Payment at signup | Monthly equivalent | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly subscription | $15 | $15 | Another $15 each month unless cancelled in time |
| Annual subscription | $120 | $10 over twelve months | Another $120 after a year unless renewal is stopped |
| Six-month pass, no renewal | $72 | $12 over six months | Access ends after six months; no automatic new charge |

If you use the program for three months, the monthly option costs **$45**, assuming you stop it before the fourth charge. You have already paid **$120** for the annual option or **$72** for the pass, even if you stop using them after month three. Unused access is not automatically a refund.

At eight monthly payments, $15 × 8 equals the annual price of $120. Twelve monthly payments total $180. The annual option therefore costs less in this example if you want more than eight months, can afford the upfront payment and use the same features. That arithmetic does not establish which real program you will use, or whether its content suits you.

## Make a billing note before entering payment details

Copy the following into a note and fill it from the final checkout, not just an advertisement:

- **Offer and seller:** exact plan name, included features and the company that will bill you.
- **Due today:** total, currency, taxes and any optional extras selected.
- **Later payments:** amount, interval and first renewal date, or explicit confirmation of no renewal.
- **Commitment:** whether installments remain payable if you stop renewal.
- **Access:** when it begins and when it ends after cancellation.
- **Exit route:** where to cancel, the applicable deadline and where to request a refund separately.

If the final checkout and sales page disagree, resolve the difference before paying. A screenshot of the selected plan and checkout terms gives you a record of the offer you actually accepted.

## A free trial can still create a future bill

The [FTC's consumer guidance on trials and automatic renewals](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions) explains that a trial requiring payment details may convert to paid access unless you cancel in time. Check the trial's end date, the amount charged afterward and the cancellation process before signing up.

Set your reminder before the provider's cutoff, with time to resolve an account or support problem. For subscriptions billed through Apple, [Apple says to cancel a free or discounted trial at least 24 hours before it ends](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428). Do not assume every other provider uses that same deadline.

A freely available exercise video with no payment agreement is a different arrangement from a trial attached to a subscription. Decide which one you mean when planning to “try it free.”

## Find who bills you before trying to cancel

Start with your receipt and account records. Watching a workout in an iPhone or Android app does not by itself identify the company collecting payment. If the subscription is missing, check which account made the purchase.

| Billing route on your receipt | Where to start | What to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apple | [Apple's subscription cancellation instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428) | The Apple Account on the receipt and the subscription's status |
| Google Play | [Google Play's subscription help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481?hl=en) | The purchasing Google Account, cancellation confirmation and any remaining committed payments |
| ClickBank | [ClickBank order support](https://www.clkbank.com/) and your order receipt | Whether you need to cancel future billing, request a refund or both |
| The workout company's website | Its official account billing page or the support route on the receipt | The exact plan, renewal status and access end date |

For an Apple-billed subscription on an iPhone, Apple's instructions start in **Settings → your name → Subscriptions**; select the subscription and follow the cancellation option. For Google Play, open its subscriptions area, select the subscription and complete the cancellation steps. Google explicitly warns that **uninstalling the app does not cancel the subscription**.

Use the billing provider's current instructions for your device and account. This is a route-finding checklist, not a claim that we have tested every company's cancellation process.

## Cancellation, refunds and access dates answer different questions

**Cancellation:** will a later renewal charge happen? **Refund:** will a payment already made be returned? **Access:** when can you no longer watch the workouts? Record each answer separately.

[ClickBank's current return and subscription policy](https://support.clickbank.com/en/articles/10535349-clickbank-s-return-and-subscription-cancellation-policy) states that cancelling recurring billing stops future charges but does not itself generate a refund. An approved refund on a recurring product also cancels that subscription. Check the particular order's return terms rather than treating an advertised return period as an unconditional promise.

Google Play generally allows use for the period already paid after cancellation; that does not establish another seller's access policy or the treatment of a trial. Check the displayed end date. If you choose a pause instead, record when billing resumes rather than assuming the plan is permanently cancelled.

Save the confirmation, request date, plan name and order reference. Check that the account reflects the requested change, and review the next statement. The FTC recommends retaining cancellation records and contacting your card issuer about charges that continue after cancellation. Follow the appropriate provider's process if something is unresolved.
