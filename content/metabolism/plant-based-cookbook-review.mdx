The Complete Plant-Based Cookbook is a digital recipe collection sold through ClickBank. This review examines its public offer and checkout, checked on September 9, 2026. We have not purchased the book, cooked its recipes or assessed the nutritional accuracy of the complete collection.

**Our assessment:** consider it if your main problem is finding plant-based meals you want to cook and you prefer a collection in one place. The basic option is the lower-cost way to obtain the cookbook. There is no reason to buy the bundle unless you expect to use its extra guides. Neither purchase is necessary to start planning meals.

## What is included and what does it cost?

The [seller's public page](https://plantbasedcookbook.com/) displayed these options:

| Option | Advertised price in USD | Advertised contents |
|---|---|---|
| Essentials | $9 | Digital cookbook with 200+ recipes and a nutrition guide |
| Full Bundle | $17 | Essentials plus a 30-day guide and collections covering smoothies, acai bowls and baking |

The seller advertises a one-time payment, digital delivery, lifetime access and a 60-day refund guarantee. It describes recipes using common ingredients, generally taking 30 minutes or less. These are seller claims; we have not timed the recipes or tested access and refunds.

## Check the checkout, not just the headline price

During our basic-option checkout inspection, ClickBank displayed a separate, optional recipe add-on. Its cost was additional to the cookbook price.

Check the selected product, currency, taxes and total before paying. A localized price may differ from the US-dollar price above. Review additional offers separately, and do not assume every extra recipe collection is part of the base purchase. Prices and packages can change after this assessment.

Keep your order confirmation and use [ClickBank customer support](https://www.clkbank.com/) for order questions. A refund promise is a purchasing term, not evidence of nutritional quality or results.

## Who might find a cookbook useful?

The practical question is whether a collection would reduce time spent deciding what to cook. Before spending anything, write down meals you would actually make, when you would prepare them and which ingredients you already have. Our free [plant-based meal-planning guide for beginners](/metabolism/plant-based-meal-planning-beginners/) shows a four-dinner example and its grocery list. If your main obstacle is a busy shopping schedule, a short grocery list may be more useful than another book.

When comparing cookbooks, look for serving sizes, ingredient substitutions, storage instructions and equipment you already own. If you track nutrients, ask whether the book includes amounts per serving and how those were calculated. The public material we inspected was insufficient to verify the complete collection against those criteria.

Choose a format you can use comfortably in the kitchen. A digital download may suit a tablet or phone; someone who prefers a printed, bound book should factor that preference into the decision.

## A recipe collection is not a weight-loss treatment

We cannot predict a weight change from purchasing or following this cookbook. Research about an eating pattern does not validate every commercial book that uses its name.

[NIDDK's guidance on evaluating weight-loss programs](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/choosing-a-safe-successful-weight-loss-program) emphasizes realistic goals, an eating plan suited to the individual, physical activity and ongoing support. A collection of recipes does not, by itself, establish that it provides those services.

If you need meals adapted for a medical condition, allergies or prescribed treatment, ask a qualified clinician or registered dietitian to help assess suitability. We have not verified this book as a meal plan for people taking GLP-1 medicines. It should not be presented as a replacement for medication or individualized nutritional care.

Our [supplement evidence guide](/supplements/supplements-for-weight-loss/) and [natural thermogenics guide](/supplements/natural-thermogenics/) cover a different purchasing decision. A cookbook offers cooking ideas; a supplement makes claims about an ingredient or formula. Evaluate each against the problem you actually want to solve.

## A free alternative to try first

Before choosing a paid resource, use our [free recipes vs paid meal plans comparison](/metabolism/free-recipes-vs-paid-meal-plans/) to identify the task you need help with and compare the total commitment.

The [NHS Healthier Families recipe library](https://www.nhs.uk/healthier-families/recipes/) provides free recipes and filters for meal type, preparation time and dietary preferences. It includes vegetarian options as well as other dishes, so it is not exclusively vegan.

Try saving a few recipes and writing a grocery list. If that gives you enough variety, you may not need a paid collection. If you still prefer an organized digital cookbook, compare the current offer below with the free resources and books you already use.
