## Introduction

Saxenda and Wegovy are both FDA-approved injectable GLP-1 receptor agonists for chronic weight management — yet they differ significantly in active drug, dosing frequency, and the magnitude of weight loss they produce. With obesity affecting more than 40% of U.S. adults, choosing between these two medications has become a clinically important decision for patients and prescribers alike.

Both drugs work by activating glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptors in the brain's appetite-regulating centers, slowing gastric emptying and reducing caloric intake. But Saxenda (liraglutide 3.0 mg, Novo Nordisk) requires a daily injection, while Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg, Novo Nordisk) is administered just once per week — and their efficacy data tell a strikingly different story.

The STEP 8 randomized clinical trial provided the first head-to-head evidence: semaglutide produced more than twice the weight loss of liraglutide over 68 weeks, with a comparable GI side effect profile and fewer treatment discontinuations. This article reviews that trial alongside the foundational SCALE and STEP 1 studies to give you a data-driven comparison of both medications.

## Drug Profiles at a Glance

| Feature | Saxenda (Liraglutide) | Wegovy (Semaglutide) |
|---|---|---|
| Drug class | GLP-1 receptor agonist | GLP-1 receptor agonist |
| FDA approval (obesity) | December 2014 | June 2021 |
| Approved dose | 3.0 mg/day subcutaneous | 2.4 mg/week subcutaneous |
| Injection frequency | Once daily | Once weekly |
| Half-life | ~13 hours | ~7 days |
| Average weight loss (trial) | −8.0% (SCALE) | −14.9% (STEP 1) |
| Starting dose | 0.6 mg/day, titrate over 5 weeks | 0.25 mg/week, titrate over 16 weeks |

The longer half-life of semaglutide — approximately 7 days versus 13 hours for liraglutide — explains the once-weekly versus once-daily dosing. Both require subcutaneous injections via a pre-filled pen, and both are used alongside diet and physical activity counseling.

## Saxenda: The SCALE Obesity Trial

The foundational evidence for Saxenda comes from the SCALE (Satiety and Clinical Adiposity — Liraglutide Evidence) Obesity and Prediabetes trial, a 56-week phase 3 RCT published in *The New England Journal of Medicine* in 2015.

> **Evidence:** "3.0 mg of once-daily subcutaneous liraglutide was associated with clinically meaningful weight loss in overweight or obese patients, with concurrent reductions in glycemic variables and multiple cardiometabolic risk factors." — *Pi-Sunyer X, et al. N Engl J Med. 2015;373(1):11-22.* [DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1411892](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1411892)

The trial enrolled 3,731 adults with a BMI ≥30 or ≥27 with a weight-related comorbidity. At 56 weeks:
- Mean weight loss: **−8.4 kg (−8.0%)** with liraglutide vs −2.8 kg with placebo
- 63% of liraglutide patients achieved ≥5% weight loss (vs 27% placebo)
- 33% achieved ≥10% weight loss (vs 10% placebo)

These results established liraglutide as a meaningful weight management tool — a meaningful advance over prior pharmacotherapy options at the time of its approval.

---

## Wegovy: The STEP 1 Trial

Wegovy's approval rests on the STEP (Semaglutide Treatment Effect in People with Obesity) program. STEP 1, published in *NEJM* in 2021, is the landmark efficacy trial.

> **Evidence:** "The mean change in body weight from baseline to week 68 was −14.9% in the semaglutide group as compared with −2.4% with placebo, for an estimated treatment difference of −12.4 percentage points." — *Wilding JPH, et al. N Engl J Med. 2021;384(11):989-1002.* [DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2032183](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)

STEP 1 enrolled 1,961 adults without diabetes. At 68 weeks:
- Mean weight loss: **−14.9% (−15.3 kg)** with semaglutide vs −2.4% with placebo
- 86% of semaglutide patients achieved ≥5% weight loss
- 69% achieved ≥10% weight loss
- 50% achieved ≥15% weight loss

The scale of weight loss was unprecedented for a non-surgical intervention at the time and triggered massive clinical interest in once-weekly semaglutide as a weight management option.

## Head-to-Head: The STEP 8 Trial

While the SCALE and STEP 1 trials used placebo controls, the STEP 8 trial directly compared both active drugs under identical conditions — making it the gold-standard evidence for the Saxenda vs Wegovy question.

> **Evidence:** "Mean body weight change from baseline to 68 weeks was −15.8% with semaglutide versus −6.4% with liraglutide (difference −9.4 percentage points; 95% CI, −11.8 to −7.0; P<0.001)." — *Wadden TA, et al. JAMA. 2022;327(2):138-150.* [DOI: 10.1001/jama.2021.23619](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.23619)

The trial randomized 338 adults with overweight or obesity (without diabetes) across 19 U.S. sites to 68 weeks of once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg, once-daily liraglutide 3.0 mg, or placebo — all combined with behavioral counseling for diet and exercise.

### STEP 8 Weight Loss Results

| Endpoint | Semaglutide 2.4 mg | Liraglutide 3.0 mg | Placebo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean weight change | −15.8% | −6.4% | −2.6% |
| Mean kg lost | −15.3 kg | −6.8 kg | −2.7 kg |
| ≥5% weight loss | 90.0% | 67.3% | 45.2% |
| ≥10% weight loss | 75.6% | 30.8% | 16.1% |
| ≥15% weight loss | 55.6% | 12.0% | 4.0% |
| ≥20% weight loss | 38.5% | 6.0% | 1.3% |

At every threshold, semaglutide significantly outperformed liraglutide. The gap widens at higher weight loss targets: roughly 6× more semaglutide patients than liraglutide patients achieved ≥20% weight loss.

### Why Does Semaglutide Produce Greater Weight Loss?

The molecular differences between the two drugs help explain the efficacy gap. Semaglutide was engineered with a fatty acid chain that binds albumin more tightly, resulting in a half-life of approximately 7 days. This sustained receptor occupancy produces more consistent appetite suppression compared to liraglutide's daily peak-and-trough pattern. Additionally, semaglutide has demonstrated stronger central nervous system effects on appetite and food reward behavior in neuroimaging studies.

## Side Effects: How Do They Compare?

Both drugs share the same GLP-1 receptor agonist side effect class — primarily gastrointestinal — and the STEP 8 trial quantified these differences directly.

> **Evidence:** "GI adverse events were reported by 84.1% of participants receiving semaglutide and 82.7% of those on liraglutide. Fewer participants stopped treatment due to adverse events with semaglutide (3.2%) than liraglutide (12.6%)." — *Wadden TA, et al. JAMA. 2022;327(2):138-150.* [DOI: 10.1001/jama.2021.23619](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.23619)

The raw GI event rates were nearly identical — approximately 84% for both. However, the critical difference is in **treatment discontinuation due to side effects**: 3.2% for semaglutide versus 12.6% for liraglutide. This suggests that while both drugs trigger similar GI symptoms, semaglutide patients tolerate them better and stay on treatment longer.

### Common Side Effects (Both Drugs)

| Side Effect | Semaglutide (STEP 8) | Liraglutide (STEP 8) |
|---|---|---|
| Nausea | ~57% | ~56% |
| Diarrhea | ~35% | ~29% |
| Constipation | ~27% | ~25% |
| Vomiting | ~25% | ~28% |
| Stopped treatment (AEs) | 3.2% | 12.6% |

Both drugs carry FDA warnings for risk of thyroid C-cell tumors (based on rodent data), pancreatitis, and gallbladder disease. Neither is recommended during pregnancy. These contraindications are class effects and apply equally to both medications.

## Systematic Review: Confirming the Efficacy Gap

A 2022 systematic review and network meta-analysis examined 23 randomized controlled trials involving 11,545 patients across multiple GLP-1 receptor agonists and weight management medications.

> **Evidence:** "Among pharmacological treatments for obesity, semaglutide 2.4 mg achieved the greatest weight loss (−12.47 kg), followed by liraglutide 3.0 mg (−5.24 kg), both significantly superior to placebo." — *Shi Q, et al. Front Endocrinol. 2022;13:1043499.* [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36510488/)

This independent, pooled analysis confirms the efficacy hierarchy seen in STEP 8: semaglutide achieves approximately 2.4× greater absolute weight loss than liraglutide in real-world trial populations.

## Cost and Access Considerations

Both Saxenda and Wegovy are manufactured by Novo Nordisk, and both carry list prices exceeding $1,000/month without insurance. However, insurance coverage differs significantly:

- **Saxenda** was approved in 2014 and has more established formulary placement at many commercial plans
- **Wegovy** was approved in 2021 and faced widespread coverage denials before increasing adoption; availability has been limited by periodic supply constraints

Both medications require a BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with at least one weight-related comorbidity, for prescription under label. Some insurers apply step-therapy requirements, meaning liraglutide may be required before semaglutide coverage is authorized.

For patients exploring coverage options, our guide to [insurance coverage for weight loss drugs](/medications/insurance-coverage-weight-loss-drugs) covers strategies for navigating prior authorization.

## Which Should You Choose?

The clinical evidence strongly favors Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg) when weight loss efficacy is the primary consideration. The STEP 8 trial showed semaglutide producing 2.5× greater weight loss than liraglutide with better tolerability (fewer treatment discontinuations) and only once-weekly dosing versus daily injections.

That said, Saxenda may be appropriate in specific scenarios:
- Insurance requires prior liraglutide trial before semaglutide approval
- Patient has a documented intolerance to semaglutide
- Cost or supply constraints make liraglutide more accessible
- Patient prefers more frequent dosing for behavioral reassurance (less common)

The decision should always involve a clinician who can account for a patient's full medical history, comorbidities, prior medication history, and insurance situation. For patients currently on Saxenda who want to know how to transition to Wegovy, the [Wegovy dosing schedule](/glp-1/wegovy-dosing-schedule) article outlines the titration protocol and what to expect in the first weeks of treatment.

## Key Takeaways

- Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg once weekly) produces approximately **2.5× greater weight loss** than Saxenda (liraglutide 3.0 mg once daily) based on the STEP 8 head-to-head trial (−15.8% vs −6.4% at 68 weeks)
- Both drugs cause similar rates of GI side effects, but **far fewer patients discontinue semaglutide** due to adverse events (3.2% vs 12.6%)
- Once-weekly dosing with Wegovy offers a **convenience advantage** over Saxenda's daily injection requirement
- Both require a BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 with comorbidity), lifestyle counseling, and a physician's prescription
- Insurance formularies and cost may influence which drug is accessible for a given patient
