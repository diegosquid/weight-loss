## Introduction

Starting a GLP-1 medication is a significant step in your weight management journey. Whether you've been prescribed semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound), or another GLP-1 receptor agonist, knowing what to expect can make the difference between success and early discontinuation.

The first few weeks are crucial. Your body is adapting to a powerful metabolic medication, and understanding the timeline of effects, potential side effects, and how to manage them will set you up for long-term success.

> **Evidence:** "Semaglutide should be initiated at 0.25 mg once weekly and injected subcutaneously without regard to meals. Dose escalation should occur after 4 weeks to doses of 0.5 mg, 1 mg, and 1.7 mg, and the maintenance dose of 2.4 mg." — *Chao & Tronieri. J Clin Pharm Ther. 2022.* [DOI: 10.1111/jcpt.13679](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpt.13679)

## Before Your First Injection

### What You'll Need

- Your prescribed medication (refrigerated until first use)
- Alcohol swabs
- Sharps container for used needles
- A journal or app to track symptoms and weight
- Emergency contact information for your healthcare provider

### Setting Expectations

GLP-1 medications are not magic pills. They work by mimicking natural gut hormones that regulate appetite, blood sugar, and digestion. The full effects build gradually over weeks to months.

> **Evidence:** "The elimination half-life of semaglutide is approximately 1 week; therefore, semaglutide will be present for approximately 5-7 weeks after the last dose. Steady state is achieved by Week 5 when injected once weekly." — *Chao & Tronieri. J Clin Pharm Ther. 2022.* [DOI: 10.1111/jcpt.13679](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpt.13679)

## Week-by-Week Timeline: What to Expect

### Weeks 1-4: The Starting Dose

**Dose:** 0.25 mg semaglutide (or 2.5 mg tirzepatide)

This initial dose is intentionally low. Its purpose is to let your body begin adapting to the medication, not to produce significant weight loss.

**What you may experience:**
- Mild appetite reduction
- Early satiety (feeling full sooner)
- Possible mild nausea or bloating
- Minimal weight change (0-2% of body weight)

**Why this matters:** The starting dose allows your gastrointestinal system to gradually adapt. GLP-1 receptors in your gut and brain need time to adjust to increased stimulation.

### Weeks 5-8: First Escalation

**Dose:** 0.5 mg semaglutide (or 5 mg tirzepatide)

**What you may experience:**
- More noticeable appetite suppression
- Increased feelings of fullness
- Possible GI side effects (nausea, constipation, or diarrhea)
- Weight loss beginning (2-4% of body weight)

> **Evidence:** "Nausea and diarrhea were the most common adverse events with semaglutide; they were typically transient and mild-to-moderate in severity and subsided with time." — *Wilding et al. N Engl J Med. 2021.* [DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2032183](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)

### Weeks 9-12: Second Escalation

**Dose:** 1.0 mg semaglutide (or 7.5 mg tirzepatide)

**What you may experience:**
- Significant appetite reduction
- Food noise (constant thoughts about food) diminishing
- More consistent weight loss (4-6% total)
- Possible need for dose adjustment if side effects are significant

### Weeks 13-16: Approaching Maintenance

**Dose:** 1.7 mg semaglutide (or 10 mg tirzepatide)

**What you may experience:**
- Therapeutic effects becoming established
- Weight loss accelerating (6-8% total)
- Side effects typically decreasing as body adapts
- New eating patterns feeling more natural

### Week 16+: Maintenance Dose

**Dose:** 2.4 mg semaglutide (or 10-15 mg tirzepatide)

This is where the full therapeutic effects are realized.

> **Evidence:** "The mean change in body weight from baseline to week 68 was −14.9% in the semaglutide group as compared with −2.4% with placebo. More participants in the semaglutide group achieved weight reductions of 5% or more (86.4%), 10% or more (69.1%), and 15% or more (50.5%)." — *Wilding et al. N Engl J Med. 2021.* [DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2032183](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)

## Common Side Effects and How to Manage Them

### Gastrointestinal Effects

GI side effects are the most common, affecting 40-70% of patients. They're usually mild to moderate and transient.

| Side Effect | Frequency | Management Strategies |
|-------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Nausea | 44-74% | Eat smaller meals; avoid high-fat foods; stay hydrated |
| Diarrhea | 25-31% | Increase fiber gradually; avoid trigger foods |
| Constipation | 11-24% | Hydration; fiber; physical activity |
| Vomiting | 10-24% | Eat slowly; stop at first sign of fullness |
| Abdominal pain | 10-20% | Smaller, more frequent meals |

> **Evidence:** "The most common adverse events were gastrointestinal and were primarily mild to moderate in severity in the tirzepatide and semaglutide groups (nausea, 17 to 22% and 18%; diarrhea, 13 to 16% and 12%; and vomiting, 6 to 10% and 8%, respectively)." — *Frías et al. N Engl J Med. 2021.* [DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2107519](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2107519)

### Strategies for Managing Nausea

1. **Eat slowly** — Take 20-30 minutes for meals
2. **Smaller portions** — Multiple small meals vs. three large ones
3. **Avoid high-fat foods** — These delay gastric emptying further
4. **Stay upright** — Don't lie down for 2-3 hours after eating
5. **Ginger or peppermint** — Natural anti-nausea remedies
6. **Consider antiemetics** — Discuss with your doctor if needed

### Other Common Effects

**Fatigue:** Some patients report tiredness, especially in the first weeks. This often improves as the body adjusts.

**Injection site reactions:** Mild redness, itching, or bruising can occur. Proper rotation of injection sites minimizes this.

**Changes in taste:** Some foods may taste different or less appealing.

## Injection Technique: Getting It Right

Proper injection technique ensures optimal medication delivery and minimizes discomfort.

### Choosing Your Injection Site

GLP-1 medications can be injected in three areas:

1. **Abdomen** — At least 2 inches (5 cm) from the navel; preferred for most patients
2. **Thigh** — Front of the thigh, at least 4 inches above the knee
3. **Upper arm** — Back of the arm; may need assistance

> **Evidence:** "Similar exposure was achieved in three subcutaneous administration sites: the abdomen, thigh, and upper arm." — *Chao & Tronieri. J Clin Pharm Ther. 2022.* [DOI: 10.1111/jcpt.13679](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpt.13679)

### The Injection Process

1. **Wash your hands** thoroughly
2. **Inspect the medication** — Check for particles or discoloration
3. **Clean the site** with an alcohol swab; let it dry
4. **Pinch the skin** gently to create a fold
5. **Insert the needle** at 90 degrees
6. **Inject slowly** — Count to 5-10 seconds
7. **Hold for 6 seconds** after the click (for pen injectors)
8. **Remove and dispose** in a sharps container

### Site Rotation

Rotate injection sites with each dose to prevent lipohypertrophy (fatty lumps) and ensure consistent absorption.

- Use a different site each week
- Stay at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) from previous injection
- Keep a simple log if needed

## Weight Loss Timeline: Setting Realistic Expectations

### Early Weeks (1-8)

Weight loss is typically modest initially. Some patients lose 2-4% of body weight in the first two months.

### Mid-Term (3-6 months)

This is where significant weight loss occurs. By month 6:
- **Semaglutide:** Average 10-12% body weight loss
- **Tirzepatide:** Average 15-18% body weight loss

> **Evidence:** "At week 20, participants who received semaglutide had lost a mean of 10.6% of their body weight from baseline." — *Garvey et al. Nat Med. 2022.* [DOI: 10.1038/s41591-022-01887-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-01887-3)

### Long-Term (6-12+ months)

Weight loss continues but at a slower rate. The STEP 1 trial showed continued loss through week 68.

| Timeframe | Expected Weight Loss (Semaglutide) | Expected Weight Loss (Tirzepatide) |
|-----------|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| 4 weeks | 2-3% | 3-4% |
| 12 weeks | 6-8% | 8-10% |
| 24 weeks | 10-12% | 14-16% |
| 52 weeks | 14-15% | 18-20% |

## When to Contact Your Healthcare Provider

### Call Immediately If You Experience:

- Severe abdominal pain (possible pancreatitis)
- Persistent vomiting preventing fluid intake
- Signs of allergic reaction (rash, difficulty breathing)
- Severe hypoglycemia symptoms (if also on diabetes medication)

### Schedule a Visit If:

- Side effects persist beyond 4 weeks at a given dose
- You're not tolerating dose escalations
- Weight loss stalls for more than 4 weeks after reaching maintenance
- You have concerns about continuing treatment

## Lifestyle Modifications: Maximizing Your Results

GLP-1 medications work best combined with lifestyle changes.

### Nutrition

- **Protein priority** — Aim for 60-80g daily to preserve muscle mass
- **Hydration** — 8-10 glasses of water daily
- **Fiber** — 25-30g daily to support gut health
- **Limit alcohol** — Can worsen GI side effects and add empty calories

### Physical Activity

> **Evidence:** "Participants received individual counseling sessions every 4 weeks to help them adhere to a reduced-calorie diet (500-kcal deficit per day) and increased physical activity (with 150 minutes per week of physical activity encouraged)." — *Wilding et al. N Engl J Med. 2021.* [DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2032183](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)

- Start with walking if new to exercise
- Aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly
- Include resistance training 2-3 times weekly to preserve muscle

### Sleep and Stress

- Prioritize 7-9 hours of sleep
- Manage stress — cortisol can counteract weight loss efforts

## What If I Miss a Dose?

**If it's been less than 5 days:** Take the missed dose as soon as you remember, then resume your regular schedule.

**If it's been more than 5 days:** Skip the missed dose and take your next dose on the scheduled day.

**Never double up** to make up for a missed dose.

## The Bottom Line

Starting a GLP-1 medication is a journey, not a quick fix. The first 16 weeks are about gradual dose escalation and allowing your body to adapt. Side effects are common but usually manageable and transient.

Success requires:
- Patience with the dose escalation process
- Proactive management of side effects
- Consistent injection technique
- Supporting lifestyle modifications
- Regular communication with your healthcare provider

The clinical data is clear: patients who persist through the adjustment period achieve significant, clinically meaningful weight loss that can transform their health.

> **Evidence:** "In participants with overweight or obesity, 2.4 mg of semaglutide once weekly plus lifestyle intervention was associated with sustained, clinically relevant reduction in body weight." — *Wilding et al. N Engl J Med. 2021.* [DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2032183](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183)
